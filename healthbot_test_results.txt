    **Understanding Diabetes: A Simple Guide**
    
    Diabetes is a common condition that affects how your body uses sugar (glucose) for energy. It occurs when your body either doesn't make enough insulin, a hormone that helps sugar enter your cells, or can't use insulin effectively. This leads to high levels of sugar in your blood, which can cause various health problems over time.
    
    ### 1. WHAT IS IT?
    - Diabetes is a condition where blood sugar levels are too high.
    - It occurs when the pancreas doesn't produce enough insulin or the body can't use insulin properly.
    
    ### 2. CAUSES & RISK FACTORS
    - Causes include genetic factors and lifestyle choices such as diet and physical activity.
    - Risk factors include being overweight, having a family history of diabetes, and being over 40 years old.
    
    ### 3. COMMON SYMPTOMS
    - Increased thirst and frequent urination.
    - Unexplained weight loss.
    - Fatigue and tiredness.
    - Blurry vision and slow-healing sores.
    
    ### 4. DIAGNOSIS & TESTS
    - Blood tests like the A1C test, fasting blood sugar test, and oral glucose tolerance test are used to diagnose diabetes.
    
    ### 5. TREATMENT OPTIONS
    - Lifestyle changes such as healthy eating and regular exercise.
    - Medications like Metformin and insulin therapy.
    - Monitoring blood sugar levels regularly.
    
    ### 6. WHICH DOCTOR TO SEE
    - An endocrinologist, who specializes in hormone-related conditions, is often involved in diabetes care.
    - Other specialists may include dietitians, podiatrists, and ophthalmologists.

    - Symptoms like extreme thirst, confusion, and fruity-smelling breath may indicate a serious condition called diabetic ketoacidosis, requiring immediate medical attention.
    
    ### 8. COMPLICATIONS
    - If left untreated, diabetes can lead to heart disease, nerve damage, kidney problems, and vision issues.
    
    ### 9. PREVENTION TIPS
